Growing Tips

Healthy plants benefit from consistent care and observation.

Start farming in an urban space

1. Choose a suitable spot

Look for a balcony, courtyard, rooftop or sunny window. Most vegetables need around 4–6 hours of sunlight; leafy greens often manage with less.

2. Start with containers

Reuse clean buckets, pots or crates that are safe for growing food. Make drainage holes so roots do not sit in water.

3. Use healthy growing media

Fill containers with a loose, well-draining potting mix enriched with mature compost. Avoid contaminated soil and containers that held chemicals.

4. Pick easy crops

Begin with crops suited to your climate and space, such as rape, spinach, lettuce, spring onions, tomatoes or herbs. Follow seed-packet spacing guidance.

5. Water wisely

Check the soil with a finger and water when the top layer begins to dry. Water near the soil in the cooler morning or evening and make sure excess water can drain.

6. Feed and observe plants

Add compost in moderation and inspect leaves for yellowing, holes or pests. Remove badly affected leaves and use safe, responsible pest-control methods.

7. Keep a simple growing record

Write down planting dates, watering, weather and harvests. This helps you learn which crops perform best in your space.

8. Harvest and replant

Harvest leafy crops a little at a time when appropriate, use clean tools, and replant small batches so you can enjoy regular produce.
