Growing Tips

Practical crop-growing advice for balconies, courtyards, rooftops, containers and other small urban spaces.

Growing crops in urban areas and small spaces

You do not need a large garden to produce fresh food. A balcony, courtyard, doorstep, rooftop or sunny windowsill can support productive containers and vertical growing spaces. Start small, choose crops suited to your conditions and make good use of every available area.

01

Choose the right crops

Start with compact, quick-growing crops that suit containers and frequent harvesting.

  • Leafy vegetables: spinach, rape, lettuce and kale.
  • Herbs: coriander, parsley, basil and spring onions.
  • Fruiting crops: cherry tomatoes, peppers and bush beans in suitably sized pots.
  • Root crops: radishes and short-rooted carrots in deeper containers.
02

Use containers and vertical space

Reuse clean buckets, pots, grow bags and sturdy crates, ensuring they are safe for food growing and have drainage holes. Place taller containers behind shorter plants so they do not block sunlight. Use trellises or strong supports for climbing beans, cucumbers and tomatoes.

03

Prepare healthy growing media

Use a light, well-draining potting mix enriched with mature compost. Avoid compacted soil from construction areas or unknown contaminated sites. Larger containers hold moisture longer and give roots more room; choose a container depth that matches the crop.

04

Make the most of sunlight

Observe your space throughout the day before planting. Fruiting crops such as tomatoes and peppers generally need several hours of direct sunlight. Leafy greens and some herbs can tolerate partial shade. Rotate containers when one side receives more light.

05

Water carefully in small spaces

Check the soil with your finger and water when the top layer begins to dry. Water near the soil rather than over the leaves, preferably in the cooler morning. Containers dry faster than garden beds, especially in hot or windy weather, so check them regularly without leaving roots waterlogged.

06

Feed, inspect and harvest

Add mature compost or use a suitable fertiliser according to its instructions. Check the underside of leaves for pests, remove damaged growth and keep the growing area tidy. Harvest leafy vegetables a little at a time to encourage continued growth, and pick crops when they are ready.

Try a simple starter garden

Begin with one container of spinach or lettuce, one pot of spring onions and one supported tomato plant if you have enough sunlight. Learn how each crop responds before expanding your garden.
